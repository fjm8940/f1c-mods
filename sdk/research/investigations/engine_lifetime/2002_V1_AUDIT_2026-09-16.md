Scope: read-only inspection of `%USERPROFILE%\Documents\F1 Challenge V10\Custom\2002\V1\SeasonData\Vehicles`. The owner reports too many retirements. This is an investigation, not a tuning change or a gameplay validation.

## Verified file evidence

All 12 team folders contain one `engine.ini`; the 24 driver `.veh` files point to their team `.hdv`, whose `[ENGINE]` section selects `Normal=Engine` (the local `engine.ini`). All four `Lifetime*` values were present in each engine file. Each custom engine file has the same SHA-256 hash as its matching `%USERPROFILE%\Documents\F1 Challenge V10\SeasonData\Vehicles\<team>\2002_*\engine.ini` counterpart. Thus these values are inherited from this installation's base 2002 season, not a V1-specific engine-file divergence.

The default `RevLimitSetting=5` in all 12 files. At that setting, the nominal limit is above the lifetime RPM base for every car; for example BAR's `RevLimitRange=(17100,100,11)` gives 17600 versus a 16760.6 base, and Ferrari's `(17600,100,11)` gives 18100 versus a 17223.6 base. Prolonged high RPM and oil temperature above the stated thresholds can consume lifetime faster, but the actual curves/average in-race RPM and temperatures were not measured here. The `.hdv` default radiator setting is 3 in all 12 files; its race setup can differ.

## Interpretation and limits

The low 2002 averages are a plausible contributor to frequent engine-wear retirements, especially BAR and Jaguar, but this inspection alone does not prove what caused the reported retirements or how many are engine failures. The reverse-engineering evidence in `mods/extended_vanilla_features/docs/ENGINE_FAILURE.md` distinguishes player and AI paths: player seed uses `LifetimeAvg` and `LifetimeVar`, AI mechanical timers use 3× those values; their drain and failure-rate-mode behavior differ. That document also corrects earlier claims in this directory about literal runtime drain and mode-2 race-length scaling. Do not equate the table's minutes with a guaranteed race-to-failure clock. Collision damage, thermal stress, other mechanical categories, and scripted events are separate possibilities.

No files in the game installation were changed. No live race, failure-reason log, session duration, `.PLR` failure-rate setting, or oil/RPM telemetry was inspected. A safe next decision is to identify the reported retirements' reason codes and race length/failure-rate mode, then run a guarded full-weekend baseline before proposing a target reliability and a versioned candidate outside the live installation. Owner judgment is required for the desired retirement distribution; do not apply a blind multiplier to every team.
